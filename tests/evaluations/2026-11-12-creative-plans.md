# Creative-direction planning checks, 12 November 2026

## Scope and method

Three fresh agents received the updated SKILL.md path and a brief. They were asked for a selected
concept, alternatives, five slide titles and compositions, and a spoken note. They were explicitly
asked to plan only and not build files or call external services. They did not receive the review's
expected answer or suspected defects. All three could read the skill, including its worked example.

These are qualitative planning probes, not a blinded creative benchmark or end-to-end deck test.
The first two use the same queue subject as the worked example, so they check mode and voice
adaptation rather than transfer to a new domain. The third uses different subject matter.
HTML feasibility is checked separately by the queue example and smoke workflow. These agents'
proposed decks were not rendered, and this report does not establish their final visual quality.

## Board pre-read

**Brief:** sober five-slide operational board pre-read. Supplied internal snapshot: ten orders
completed picking, six reached packing, four await transfer. No time-series, staffing-capacity,
customer-story or ROI evidence. Ask for approval to investigate the handoff before deciding on
more picking capacity.

**Observed plan:** selected “Make the handoff visible.” Considered an evidence ledger and following
one order; rejected an invented order history. Led with the approval request, used a ten-unit
state display, separated observations from unknowns, proposed an investigation and ended on the
decision. It distinguished reaching packing from completed dispatch.

**Representative title:** “The snapshot locates the waiting orders but does not establish the
constraint.”

**Spoken excerpt:** “We can see where four orders are waiting. We cannot tell from this snapshot
how long they have waited, whether this pattern repeats, or what causes it.”

**Assessment:** the concept serves a decision and keeps the evidence boundary. The document
sequence is answer-first and self-contained. It still needs real slide composition and render review.

## Live teaching

**Brief:** lively five-slide lesson for new operations trainees using the same supplied snapshot,
with the cause unknown. Voice sample: “Show me where it stops. Then we can talk about fixing it.”
Include a useful learner task; no additional measured results or customer anecdotes.

**Observed plan:** selected “Show me where it stops.” Proposed regrouping the same ten order
symbols rather than duplicating ten before and ten after. Used the handoff space to separate
checkpoints, explicitly labelled the cause unknown, and gave learners the task “Point to the
queue. State one fact. Name one thing to check next.” Kept proposed investigations separate from
supplied facts.

**Representative title:** “Picking complete. Transfer still pending.”

**Assessment:** the same material produced a participatory sequence and shorter language rather
than the board plan's decision tables. The supplied voice sample influenced the opening and notes.
This is mode adaptation, not evidence of a new visual concept for an unrelated subject.

## Ceramics retailer pitch

**Brief:** distinctive five-slide pitch for an independent ceramics studio. Supplied facts:
hand-thrown mugs with visible differences in shape. Available photograph descriptions: hands
shaping wet clay, a row of different mugs, a glazed mug on a table. Voice sample: “Leave the wobble.
That's the bit I like.” Ask for a small trial display, with no quantities or terms agreed. No
sales evidence, testimonials, environmental certifications or market figures.

**Observed plan:** selected “Leave the wobble.” Proposed a straight layout baseline against the
actual variation in the photographed mugs. Considered “From hands to table” but noted that the
photos could not establish they show the same piece; considered a shelf concept but identified
its need for a labelled mockup. Planned a quote-and-product opening, a process photograph, an
inspectable row of mugs, a product detail and a plainly proposed display trial. Rejected decorative
squiggles and artificial crooked typography. Kept crops and annotations provisional until the
photos could be inspected.

**Representative titles:** “Put them together. You can see the differences.” / “Let's try a small
display.”

**Assessment:** this plan transferred to a different, image-led concept and preserved the maker's
supplied voice. It did not invent customer demand, ecological claims or commercial terms. The
photographs were described rather than inspected, so asset choices remain conditional.

## Implications and next evaluation

The revised guidance produced plausible concept selection, different mode-specific sequences and
presenter-sensitive language in these probes. This is encouraging planning evidence, not proof
that future Claude decks will be consistently distinctive.

Next, run the existing benchmark briefs through actual HTML production and review the rendered
outputs, particularly scientific explanation, supplied brand templates, dense reading decks and
single-slide revisions. Keep factual fidelity, explanatory value, identity, voice and readability
separate. Retain a familiar layout when it works better; do not reward novelty that obscures evidence.
