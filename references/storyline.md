# Storyline craft

The brief canvas and gates live in frameworks.md; headline and wording rules in writing.md. This
file is about shaping the argument.

## 1. From material to one sentence

Collect the real material first (numbers, sources, interviews, photos, past decks). If the user has
a past deck on the subject, build from it: keep content, order and phrasing; change only stale facts
and the cuts. Then write the governing thought in one sentence. Can't? The material is not ready;
say what is missing.

## 2. Ghost deck

Only titles, in order, before any design. Answer first (document) or question-led (talk). The
deck's titles, read top to bottom, are its executive summary (document) or its plot (talk).
Agree it with the user before building anything longer than ~10 slides when they are available.

## 3. Archetypes and their spines

| Archetype | Spine | Slides |
|---|---|---|
| Recommendation | answer · situation · complication · analysis by key line · options + scorecard · recommendation · financials · roadmap · risks · ask | 10-15 + appendix |
| Pitch | hook · problem · insight · solution · how it works · market (bottom-up) · traction · model · competition · team · ask | 10-12 |
| Personal story | one-line hook · 3-4 chapters each with a number · how I work · first 90 days · close | 6-9 |
| Review | scorecard vs target · what moved and why · deep dives · risks and asks · next period | 6-10 |
| Explainer | objectives · hook · concept · worked example · practice · recap | 8-15 |
| Research readout | question · method · one finding per slide · implications · limits · contribution | 10-20 |
| Change / workshop | why we're here · what changes · who is affected · process · discussion · owners | 8-15 |
| Marketing plan | insight · idea · creative · channels · timeline · KPIs · results | 8-14 |
| Event | welcome · agenda · highlights · people · thanks · what's next | 6-12 |
| Options-first | criteria · options · scorecard · case for the winner · risks | 5-8 |

Slide-by-slide versions for 40+ situations: deck-skeletons.md. Structures (pyramid, SCQA,
sparkline): frameworks.md §2.

## 4. Case competitions

- Judges read the executive summary and the ask hardest; write both last, from the content.
- Every judging criterion (insight, feasibility, impact, creativity, financials, risk) gets a titled
  slide; keep the criteria map in the exec-summary notes.
- Financials: one model in `facts` (revenue, costs, profit, cumulative cash, payback, peak funding)
  with sensitivities on the two assumptions that move the answer most.
- Risks: 3-5, each with likelihood, impact and a named mitigation and owner.
- Implementation: Gantt or milestones with owners; the critical path highlighted.
- Appendix: backup for every number a judge might challenge, titled as claims too.
- Differentiate on look as well as argument: most teams submit identical navy decks; consulting
  grammar on editorial-warm or heritage-india is remembered.

## 5. Interviews and personal stories

- Open with the value you create in one line, not a CV. A real moment beats a statistic.
- Three stories maximum. Each: situation in one line, what you did, the number that moved, what it
  says about how you work. Vary them (one quote-led, one data-led, one still in progress).
- Close with your first 30/60/90 days in their context.
- Talk mode; your own voice; ≤ 40 words a slide; the notes are your script; rehearse to time.

## 6. Source → summary → slides (papers, reports, long documents)

1. Read the whole source. Extract: citation, thesis, key points, evidence (with page numbers),
   population or sample, limitations, implications, open questions.
2. Write the summary as a short document first; check it against the source.
3. Choose the audience register: business (gloss every domain term, state each chart's metric and
   baseline in plain words) or technical (keep the field's terms).
4. One slide per distinct finding (distinct = different magnitude, place, mechanism or period).
5. Charts reproduced from the source keep the source's numbers and cite the page.
6. A limitations slide when the source has real limits (the honest slide builds trust).
